## Findings by dimension

### 1. Correctness

- **Fixed, verified independently:** `weight` reservation (BUILD-SPECS.md:221) now matches `ALGORITHM-v0.2-pathway-learner.md:145`'s `reach_weight(s,n) = ∏_{p∈prereqs(s)} P(mastery[p]≥θ)` exactly, and `DATA-LAYER.md:71`'s port signature `reach_weight(self, s: str, state) -> float` corroborates it (no weight argument anywhere). `test_weight_is_reserved` (:244) is well-targeted. This was round 2's headline Correctness defect; it is genuinely closed.
- **Unresolved from round 2: the additive z-score priority formula is still undefined at `|frontier|=1`.** BUILD-SPECS.md:225-226, `priority(e→P) = z(confidence(e)|frontier) + z((θ−ĉ_mastery[P])/SE[P]|frontier)` — a population z-score requires a nonzero sample SD. A BFS frontier with exactly one child (a skill with one prereq, or a composite with one constituent — a common, not edge-case, shape at shallow depth or in a sparse curriculum) yields `SD=0` for both terms, i.e. `0/0`. No fallback is stated (contrast `significant()`, §2, which is total on its domain). This is the same defect round 2 flagged (finding 2, blocking item 6) and it is carried forward verbatim — not narrowed, not acknowledged.
- **New: the "aging bump" unit is ambiguous.** BUILD-SPECS.md:232, "aging bump per skipped round (+1σ priority)." `priority(e→P)` is a **sum of two population z-scores**, not itself z-scored — its own empirical spread across a frontier is not 1 by construction (if the two terms are roughly independent unit-variance quantities, the sum's SD is closer to `√2`). "+1σ" could mean (a) a fixed `+1` in the same raw units as the formula's individual `z(...)` terms, or (b) `+1` empirical standard deviation of the *combined* `priority` distribution over the current frontier (which would have to be recomputed per frontier, and is a different, non-constant quantity round to round). The spec does not disambiguate, and the choice changes both the guaranteed by-when-confirmed bound and the answer to "does this bump ever violate 'no static constant may enter this formula'" (interpretation (a) is a literal, non-evidence-derived constant added to the priority the very next sentence forbids adding to; interpretation (b) is evidence-derived but expensive/underspecified to compute).
- **New: the "starvation-proof" and "bounded number of triggers" claims (BUILD-SPECS.md:229, :234) hold only for a fixed backlog, not under sustained arrival.** Aging guarantees any *individual* candidate's priority eventually dominates a *fixed* population of competing candidates (since aging accumulates without cap while ordinary z-scores are drawn from a roughly bounded practical range). But nothing bounds how many **new** significant candidates can arise per trigger — the amendment explicitly removes the type short-circuit (both `prereq` and `part_of` collected, BUILD-SPECS.md:223), and no fan-in cap is stated to extend to authored/`hard`/`part_of` edges (§5.1's "capped fan-in," `ALGORITHM-v0.2-pathway-learner.md:129`, is stated only for *growth-inserted* `prereq` edges). If the sustained per-trigger arrival rate of new significant candidates exceeds the ~3-candidates/trigger the budget clears (see Completeness/adversarial pass below), the backlog — and hence the actual wait experienced by any given candidate, including freshly-arriving ones — can grow without bound over a run, even though no *individual* candidate is mathematically excluded forever. "Never starved" and "bounded delay" are not the same claim, and the amendment states the stronger one without support.

## Strongest adversarial objection

Round 2's compound objection was: a `part_of`/`prereq` edge whose confidence `g.decay_edges()` has already driven low is (a) still a fully eligible *candidate* (confidence never gates candidacy) and (b) systematically pushed toward the back of the confirmation queue (confidence lowers priority) — so the system keeps re-diagnosing exactly the prereq path it has already learned isn't causal, while under-serving it on confirmation. Round 3's aging + evidence-only-expiry mechanism was built to close the *queue* half of this. It does — but only against a **static, one-shot** backlog. It does not close, and does not even acknowledge, the **dynamic** version of the same objection: because the amendment collects across both edge types with no short-circuit (BUILD-SPECS.md:223) and no fan-in cap is stated to extend to authored/`hard`/`part_of` edges, the number of significant candidates arising per trigger has no stated ceiling, while the number confirmed per trigger is hard-capped at `b_conf/n_conf = 3` (with the *default* parameters). If the sustained rate of new significant candidates at or above 3/trigger is not a rare pathology but the *ordinary* operating regime for any curriculum with moderate branching (which a spec introducing "collect across both types, never drop a candidate" as its central selling point should expect to be common, not exceptional), then the backlog does not merely delay individual candidates — it grows, indefinitely, across the life of a run. And it grows in a **biased** direction: the low-`confidence` (already-suspect) edges are the ones whose `z(confidence(e)|frontier)` term is lowest, so they are disproportionately represented among whichever candidates are still waiting when the backlog is non-trivial, while freshly-arriving, high-confidence `part_of` edges (born, per the amendment's own framing, without any stated low default) systematically jump ahead of them. The aging bump is the *only* countervailing force, and its own magnitude is ambiguously specified (Correctness finding 2) — so it is not even possible to check, from the spec as written, whether aging actually wins this race against a plausible arrival rate, or merely slows the bias without reversing it. This is the same structural failure family the amendment's own provenance note warns against (BUILD-SPECS.md:215, "hardcoded traversal bias fossilizes") — except here the bias is not a hardcoded weight but an emergent property of combining an unbounded-arrival, capacity-bounded-service queue with a priority function that correlates with the very quantity (`confidence`) the system uses to signal "already-diagnosed-and-rejected." No dimension above states this in its full compound form; each half (the open `tau_traverse` advisory, and the queueing-bound gap) is noted separately under Red-team/Correctness, but their multiplicative interaction — a growing backlog that is *not* neutral but systematically starves exactly the edges base B2's outcome-feedback loop most needs revisited — is the strongest single objection to round 3 as written.

## Verdict

**needs-revision**

Round 3 made real, independently-verified progress (62 → 64): the false `weight` citation is genuinely corrected, the confirmation budget is genuinely defined and tested, and the `GraphStore` port + Plug-point gap is genuinely closed. But two of round 2's six numbered blocking items were left completely unaddressed, one was only partially addressed, and this round's own re-verification surfaced a new critical-dimension defect (the queueing/backlog-growth gap in the "starvation-proof" claim) that is at least as serious as what it replaces. Specific blocking changes required to clear 80:

1. **Close or explicitly defer the `confidence`-floors-candidacy decision (round-2 item 5, still open).** Either add a bounded `edge.confidence ≥ tau_traverse`-style candidacy guard (closing the base B2 decision record's advisory), or add one sentence explicitly deferring it with a stated reason. Silence is not an acceptable third option on a review's second consecutive round.
2. **Handle the `|frontier|=1` degenerate z-score case (round-2 item 6, still open).** State the fallback (e.g., a single candidate is always visited/confirmed regardless of priority) and add a regression test.
3. **Prove or retract the "bounded delay"/"starvation-proof" claim under sustained candidate arrival, not just a fixed backlog.** Either (a) add an explicit cap on candidates-per-trigger (e.g., extend §5.1's fan-in cap to `part_of`/authored edges) so the arrival rate is provably bounded relative to `b_conf`/`n_conf`, or (b) state plainly that the guarantee is "no permanent exclusion" rather than "bounded delay," and add a test demonstrating backlog behavior under sustained above-capacity arrival (not just a one-shot excess).
4. **Disambiguate the aging-bump unit** ("+1σ priority" — fixed raw units, or the frontier priority distribution's own empirical SD) and reconcile it with the adjacent "no static constant may enter this formula" rule.
5. **Specify the floor-allocation order when `b_conf` cannot cover every candidate's `n_conf` floor.** State explicitly whether priority order governs which candidates receive the floor first (not just the remainder), and add a test.
6. **Name the concrete acyclicity-check mechanism and its cross-gate dependency.** Cross-reference `DATA-LAYER.md §6.2`'s `GraphStore.merge`/`MergeReport.rejected` explicitly (or resolve the `add_skill`-vs-`merge` ambiguity in `DATA-LAYER.md` itself first), and flag that this amendment's insertion-time acyclicity guarantee currently depends on an unapproved, concurrently-gated section.
7. **Back the `b_conf=15`/`n_conf=5` defaults with a worked candidate-volume example** showing they are calibrated to expected, not merely convenient, per-trigger candidate counts — the amendment's own design (collect across both types, no short-circuit) makes "more than 3 candidates per trigger" a foreseeably common case, not a stress-test tail.
