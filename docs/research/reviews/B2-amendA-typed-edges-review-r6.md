## Findings by dimension

### 1. Correctness

- **The `q_edge` sampling-unit fix is precise and internally verified.** `:222`'s Bernoulli(`q_edge`) per-edge-per-walk draw gives expected re-admission latency `E[trials] = 1/q_edge = 1/0.1 = 10` walks (standard geometric-distribution mean) — matches the stated "~`1/q_edge` walks." No error found.
- **`queue_rank`'s aging cap is algebraically correct and does what it claims.** `queue_rank(c) = gap_z(c) + min(age(c), A_cap)`, eviction takes the candidate with the *minimum* `queue_rank` (`:228`, "evicting the minimum of the formal queue rank"). For an aged candidate to survive eviction against a fresh candidate (`age=0`), it needs `gap_z(aged) + min(age,A_cap) > gap_z(fresh)`, i.e. `gap_z(fresh) − gap_z(aged) < min(age,A_cap) ≤ A_cap`. So a fresh arrival whose severity exceeds the aged candidate's by more than `A_cap` (3 z-units) always wins eviction — exactly the stated claim ("an aged moderate candidate can outrank at most `A_cap` z-units of fresh severity — beyond that, the far-more-severe arrival wins," `:234`). Verified correct, no sign error.
- **The arrival-damping reattribution (`:228`) is now checked directly against round 3's original objection and matches it.** Round 3's stated concern (`B2-amendA-typed-edges-review-r3.md:52`, "nothing bounds how many new significant candidates can arise per trigger... no fan-in cap is stated") is about fresh-candidate volume from the no-type-short-circuit design — `:228`'s new text explicitly names this as what `Q_max`+eviction+aging bounds, and explicitly disclaims that `τ_traverse`/`q_edge` do this job ("damps only *re-arrival from already-discredited edges*"). This is now internally consistent with `:222`'s own description of above-floor edges being "walked normally" (i.e., every trigger, undamped) — the contradiction round 5 flagged between these two clauses is resolved.
- **New, load-bearing internal contradiction introduced by this round's own eviction-comparator formalization.** `:228`'s first sentence describes floor allocation as "candidates take the `n_conf` floor... **in priority order** until `b_conf` is exhausted; uncovered candidates are re-queued with an aging bump... (explicitly **counteracting the confidence-ranked ordering bias** — an aged **low-confidence** candidate overtakes fresh **high-confidence** ones deterministically)." This framing requires the underlying floor-allocation order to include a confidence term (consistent with `priority(e→P) = z(confidence(e)|frontier) + z(gap|frontier)`, `:225-226`, which does). But `:234` states: "`gap_z` deliberately excludes the confidence term (eviction cannot compound the confidence-ranking bias); **the same `queue_rank` orders floor allocation**." `queue_rank` has **no confidence term at all** (`gap_z` + capped `age`, `:230-232`). If `queue_rank` truly governs floor allocation, there is no "confidence-ranked ordering bias" for aging to counteract in the first place — the two sentences describe mutually exclusive mechanisms for the *same* allocation step, and `test_confirmation_floor_and_aging` (`:246`, "the +1 z-unit aging overtakes confidence ranking") repeats the confidence-inclusive framing, three-way corroborating that this is a real, non-trivial contradiction rather than a stray word choice. A developer cannot determine from this text whether floor allocation is ordered by `priority(e→P)` (confidence-inclusive) or `queue_rank` (confidence-exclusive) — the two produce materially different confirmation order when edge confidence and mastery-gap severity diverge.

## Strongest adversarial objection

Round 6 formalizes `queue_rank(c) = gap_z(c) + min(age(c), A_cap)` specifically to close round 5's adversarial finding (unbounded aging → unbounded priority inversion) for the occupancy queue, and the algebra checks out for that queue (Correctness, above). But the same paragraph (`:228`) also states, unchanged since round 4, that the **confirmation-budget floor allocation** — a functionally distinct queue governing which candidates get the `n_conf` held-out floor within the `b_conf` per-trigger budget, not which candidates stay in the `Q_max` backlog — is ordered with an aging bump described as "**counteracting the confidence-ranked ordering bias**," language that only makes sense if that ordering includes a confidence term (i.e., it is `priority(e→P)`, not `queue_rank`). If that reading is correct, the confirmation-floor aging bump is the **same uncapped "+1 z-unit per skipped trigger" mechanism round 5 red-teamed** — it never appears with an `A_cap`-style cap anywhere in the text describing it, and the Parameters line (`:238`) lists "aging bump (+1 z-unit per skipped trigger, persisted across evictions)" and "`A_cap` aging cap (3 z-units)" as **two separate bullet items**, not one bounded mechanism, reinforcing that they may be genuinely distinct. Under this reading, round 5's adversarial finding is not closed so much as **relocated**: an old, only-moderately-severe candidate can no longer be evicted from the backlog forever (the occupancy fix works), but it can still, in principle, unboundedly outrank a fresh, far-more-severe candidate for the *scarcer* and more consequential resource — the actual held-out confirmation slots that determine which candidate gets redirected to first. The one sentence that could resolve this either way — "the same `queue_rank` orders floor allocation" (`:234`) — directly contradicts the "confidence-ranked ordering bias" framing it is meant to clarify (Correctness, above), so the text does not actually tell the reader which of these two readings is intended, and neither the tests (`test_confirmation_floor_and_aging` vs. `test_aging_cap_bounds_inversion`/`test_eviction_cannot_starve_forever`) nor the Parameters block resolve it. This is not raised in full in any of the nine dimensions above (Correctness and Consistency note the textual contradiction itself; this is the compound, mechanism-level consequence — that round 5's adversarial finding may only be half-closed, with the half that's closed being the less consequential one).
